Nestled in the heart of Bloomsbury, London, University College London has stood for nearly two centuries — a place where ambition meets tradition, where the brightest minds come to prove themselves against the weight of history.

Founded in 1826, UCL was the first university in England to open its doors regardless of religion or background. Its grand portico, its echoing halls, its libraries stacked floor to ceiling — all of it carries the quiet pressure of expectation.

This is where students dream of arriving. And where, once inside, they discover that keeping up is a different kind of battle entirely.

History & Founding

1826
The founding. Established as the "University of London" by a group of reformers determined to create a secular alternative to Oxford and Cambridge. No religious tests, no class barriers. Radical for its time.
1828
Doors open. The first students arrive at the Gower Street building — the grand portico already becoming iconic. Early years are financially turbulent but intellectually alive.
1836
Renamed UCL. The University of London becomes a separate examining body; the institution renames itself University College London to avoid confusion.
1878
Women admitted. UCL becomes the first university in England to admit women on equal terms as men — a full forty years before women could vote.
1940s
Wartime. WWII disrupts campus life. Parts of UCL are evacuated to Aberystwyth and Cambridge. The Bloomsbury campus sustains bomb damage; the Wilkins Building survives intact.
2000s–present
Expansion. UCL grows into one of the world's top universities, expanding into King's Cross (UCL East), Stratford, and beyond. Student population swells past 40,000.
